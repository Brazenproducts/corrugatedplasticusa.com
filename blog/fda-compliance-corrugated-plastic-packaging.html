FDA Compliance Requirements for Corrugated Plastic Food Packaging in the US

Corrugatedplasticusa Editorial • September 2026

Why FDA Compliance Matters for Corrugated Plastic Packaging

Corrugated plastic packaging used in food processing, produce handling, and cold-chain distribution must meet applicable regulatory requirements for food-contact materials. In the United States, that means alignment with FDA 21 CFR regulations governing polymeric food-contact materials. For procurement managers sourcing corrugated plastic for food applications, understanding these requirements — and how to verify compliance through supplier documentation — is essential for avoiding regulatory risk and maintaining audit readiness under SQF, BRC, FSSC 22000, and USDA inspection programs.

The Regulatory Framework: FDA 21 CFR 177.1520

Polypropylene (PP) used in food-contact applications in the United States is regulated primarily under 21 CFR 177.1520, "Olefin Polymers." This regulation specifies base resin formulation requirements and permitted additives for polypropylene in food-contact use. Key requirements include:

  • PP resin must meet specifications for base polymer composition as defined in 21 CFR 177.1520(c)
  • Additives — stabilizers, colorants, lubricants — must be from substances approved under applicable CFR sections
  • Migration limits apply for specific application conditions, including temperature, contact duration, and food type

Corrugated plastic boxes manufactured from virgin PP with compliant additive packages generally meet these requirements without further action. Non-compliance risk typically arises from recycled content (which may contain non-food-grade additives), non-compliant colorant selection, or unapproved printing inks on food-contact surfaces.

Verifying Compliance Through Supplier Documentation

When sourcing corrugated plastic boxes from Elipacko or any supplier for food applications, request the following documentation:

  • Resin Certificate of Compliance: Confirms the PP resin grade and additive package meet 21 CFR 177.1520 or equivalent standard (EU 10/2011 if exporting to Europe)
  • Material Safety Data Sheet (SDS): Identifies base resin and additive composition
  • Third-party migration testing (where required): For high-temperature applications above 40°C contact or steam sanitization cycles, migration testing under conditions-of-use may be required
  • Manufacturer quality certificate: ISO 9001 or equivalent quality management certification confirms consistent manufacturing process control

USDA Requirements for Meat and Poultry Contact Applications

For operations under USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) oversight — meat packing, poultry processing, or egg product handling — corrugated plastic containers must also meet USDA sanitizability requirements. USDA-approved packaging for direct food contact must be:

  • Made from materials that can be effectively cleaned and sanitized to prevent microbial harborage
  • Free from crevices, pits, or surface defects that resist cleaning
  • Resistant to the sanitizing agents used in the facility (bleach, quaternary ammonium compounds, peracetic acid)

PP corrugated boxes with virgin resin, smooth skin surface, and no exposed cut-flute edges in food-contact areas meet USDA sanitizability requirements in most inspection scenarios. Consult with your FSIS inspector before deployment in direct meat-contact applications.

FSMA Produce Safety Rule Considerations

Under the FDA Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) Produce Safety Rule (21 CFR Part 112), reusable containers used in covered produce operations must be:

  • Adequately cleaned before each use to protect against contamination
  • Not be a source of contamination for covered produce
  • Maintained to protect against contamination throughout their service life

The Produce Safety Rule does not prescribe specific container materials, but the cleaning and sanitization requirement means containers must be washable and must not harbor pathogens. PP corrugated — with its non-porous skin surface and compatibility with approved food-safe sanitizers — is well-suited to FSMA-compliant reusable container programs.

Audit Readiness: Documentation You Need on File

For operations subject to SQF, BRC, FSSC 22000, or similar food-safety certification audits, your packaging documentation file should include:

  • Supplier certificate of compliance for food-contact resin (21 CFR 177.1520)
  • Material specification sheet confirming FDA compliance
  • Cleaning and sanitation SOP for reusable packaging with agent concentrations and contact times
  • ATP or microbial monitoring records for the cleaning program
  • Approved supplier list entry with qualification documentation and audit date
