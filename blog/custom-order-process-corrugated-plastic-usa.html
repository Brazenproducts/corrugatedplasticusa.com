How to Place a Custom Corrugated Plastic Order: A Step-by-Step Guide for US Buyers

Corrugatedplasticusa Editorial • October 2026

Why Custom Matters in Corrugated Plastic Sourcing

Standard stock sizes cover a wide range of applications, but most B2B procurement teams eventually hit a requirement that off-the-shelf corrugated plastic cannot satisfy. A produce box that needs to match an existing rack slot dimension, a partitioned tote sized to a specific SKU, a sheet cut to fit a proprietary conveyor system — these are everyday needs in distribution, food processing, and manufacturing environments that demand custom work.

Understanding the custom order process before you start the conversation with a manufacturer saves significant time and reduces the risk of specification errors that cause delays or rejected product. This guide walks US buyers through the full process from initial inquiry to delivery.

Step 1: Define Your Specification Package

Before contacting any manufacturer, document your requirements in a written spec package. This does not need to be a formal engineering drawing, but it should cover:

  • Dimensions: Interior length × width × depth in inches (or mm if your internal system uses metric). For boxes, specify whether dimensions are interior or exterior — this affects wall thickness calculations.
  • Material gauge: PP corrugated sheet is typically available in 2mm, 3mm, 4mm, 5mm, and 7mm flute thickness. Heavier product loads and stacking requirements push toward 5–7mm.
  • Flute direction: Flutes running parallel to the length provide better compression strength; flutes running perpendicular provide better flex resistance. For most box applications, flutes parallel to the box height is standard.
  • Color: Natural (translucent), black, white, and custom colors are all available. Color also has functional uses — black adds UV resistance for outdoor staging areas.
  • Print requirements: Single-color flexo print, multi-color, or no print.
  • Closure type: Stapled, stitched, snap-lock die-cut, or open-top tote.
  • Quantity: Both the initial order quantity and your expected annual volume. Annual volume affects pricing more than initial order size.

Step 2: Request a Quotation and Sample

Submit your spec package to the manufacturer and request both a formal quotation and a pre-production sample. Reputable manufacturers will provide an engineering review with any dimensional feedback before committing to tooling. This is the stage where a manufacturer might flag that your specified interior dimension requires a non-standard flute cut that adds cost, or suggest a minor dimension adjustment that reduces material waste and lowers unit price.

For custom work sourced from a direct manufacturer like corrugated plastic boxes from Elipacko, samples are typically produced within 7–14 business days of specification confirmation. Elipacko manufactures in Thailand and Vietnam, which are not subject to the Section 301 additional duties (0% ADD) that significantly affect Chinese-origin corrugated plastic — a meaningful cost factor for US importers comparing supplier bids.

Step 3: Sample Review and Specification Sign-Off

When the pre-production sample arrives, evaluate it against your spec package systematically. Check all dimensions with a tape measure and calipers. Load-test the box to your required stack weight. Run it through your wash protocol if sanitation is a requirement. Check that print registration is accurate and ink adhesion is solid.

If changes are needed, document them in writing and request a revised sample. Do not proceed to production approval until you have a sample that fully meets your specification. Changes after production approval are expensive and slow.

Once the sample is approved, sign off in writing with the manufacturer. This written approval is your protection if delivered product deviates from the sample.

Step 4: Confirm MOQ, Lead Time, and Logistics

Custom corrugated plastic orders from overseas manufacturers typically carry minimum order quantities in the range of 500–2,000 units depending on box size and complexity. For large, simple sheet products, MOQs can be lower. For complex die-cut boxes with print, MOQs are higher because tooling and setup costs need to be amortized.

Lead times for custom orders generally run 25–45 days from production approval, plus ocean freight transit of 18–28 days to US West Coast ports. Air freight is available for urgent first orders but adds significant cost. Factor these lead times into your inventory planning — most buyers place custom orders with 60–75 days of lead time buffer built into their replenishment schedule.

Confirm Incoterms with your supplier. FOB origin (FOB Bangkok or FOB Ho Chi Minh City) means you arrange and pay for ocean freight. CIF means the supplier arranges freight to a named US port. Each has cost and control tradeoffs. Most experienced importers prefer FOB so they control carrier selection and can negotiate their own freight rates at volume.

Step 5: Customs Clearance and Receiving

Corrugated plastic boxes from Thailand or Vietnam enter the US under HTS heading 3923.10 or 3926.90 depending on form factor. Confirm the correct HTS classification with your customs broker before the first shipment — misclassification is a common source of delays and unexpected duty bills.

A customs bond, ISF filing (10+2), and a licensed customs broker are required for commercial imports. If you are new to importing, your freight forwarder can refer you to a licensed broker. For repeat orders, much of this becomes routine.

The Elipacko USA product line includes standard documentation packages — commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and material safety data sheet — that support smooth customs clearance and help satisfy FDA facility registration requirements for food-contact applications.

What to Expect on Repeat Orders

After your first successful custom order, repeat orders become significantly simpler. Your tooling is already on file, your specification is documented, and your customs broker has the commodity established in their system. Lead times shorten because there is no sample cycle. Many buyers move to blanket purchase orders with scheduled release quantities to smooth out lead time and cash flow.

The custom corrugated plastic order process has a meaningful learning curve on the first cycle. Buyers who invest in getting the specification right, building a relationship with a quality manufacturer, and establishing their import process correctly are positioned to run a reliable, cost-efficient reusable packaging program for years.
