FDA and USDA Compliance for Corrugated Plastic in Food Packaging

Corrugatedplasticusa Editorial • October 2026

Compliance Is a Procurement Requirement, Not an Afterthought

For food, beverage, and agricultural operations in the United States, packaging that contacts product must satisfy federal food-safety standards. Corrugated plastic has become a preferred reusable material precisely because polypropylene can be manufactured from food-grade resin, cleaned repeatedly, and documented for audit. Procurement teams that treat compliance as a gating specification avoid costly recalls, rejected loads, and failed third-party audits down the line.

Two regulatory frameworks matter most. The FDA governs food-contact materials under 21 CFR, which defines the resins and additives permitted for direct and indirect food contact. The USDA oversees meat, poultry, and egg facilities and expects packaging that supports sanitary handling. Buyers should require written confirmation that the corrugated plastic resin meets 21 CFR requirements before placing volume orders.

Resin Grade and Material Documentation

The foundation of compliance is the resin itself. Food-grade polypropylene must be free of prohibited additives and manufactured under controlled conditions. Elipacko produces corrugated plastic from FDA-compliant resin and can supply material documentation to support your food-safety program. As a direct manufacturer rather than a reseller, Elipacko controls the resin selection and production process, which simplifies traceability compared with sourcing through intermediaries.

When specifying, request the following: confirmation of FDA-compliant resin, any relevant migration or additive statements, and lot traceability. These documents become part of your supplier approval file and are often requested during SQF, BRC, or customer audits. Buyers evaluating the full range can review corrugated plastic boxes from Elipacko that are engineered for food-contact applications.

Hygienic Design Features

Compliance is not only about resin chemistry; it is also about physical design. Food-grade corrugated plastic packaging should minimize crevices and flat internal surfaces where debris and bacteria can accumulate. Smooth twin-wall sheet, rounded or sealed edges, and drainage-friendly geometry all support effective cleaning.

  • Non-porous polypropylene surface that resists moisture absorption
  • Sealed or capped flute ends to prevent product entrapment
  • Smooth interior walls for fast washdown and sanitizing
  • Color-coding options to separate raw and ready-to-eat zones

Cleaning Validation and Reuse

Reusable packaging only maintains compliance if it is cleaned and inspected between uses. Polypropylene tolerates the hot water, detergents, and sanitizers used in commercial washing lines without degrading, which is a key advantage over waxed fiber board. Facilities should document their wash cycle temperature, chemistry, and inspection criteria, and retire any unit showing cracks or persistent soiling. A documented cleaning protocol paired with compliant material closes the loop on food-safety expectations.

The practical compliance checklist is short but non-negotiable: verify FDA-compliant resin, collect material documentation, confirm hygienic design, validate your cleaning protocol, and keep retirement criteria for worn units.
